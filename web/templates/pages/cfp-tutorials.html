A demo video is required. Every tutorial proposal must include a 2–5 minute demo video (public or unlisted link) showing the working prototype, the workflow or what participants will build. The submission form does not accept a tutorial without it.

Format

Deliver a 5-minute presentation in the morning, followed by a 90-minute (1.5-hour) workshop in the afternoon. The morning presentation introduces your work and what participants will build; the workshop lets them build it.

Workshop size and equipment

Authors choose the size of their workshop. If the workshop needs equipment, such as robots, boards, sensors or kits, prepare the corresponding number of sets for participants to share:

Workshop sizeEquipment sets (if needed)
25 participants10 sets
50 participants20 sets

Authors are responsible for preparing all necessary equipment and environment set-ups for the workshop, while attendees are asked to bring their own laptops.

Laptop hardware requirements

{{icon "laptop"}}

Authors provide the hardware requirements for participants' laptops, such as CPU, memory (RAM), GPU, free disk space and operating system. The requirements are published on the workshop pages, so that participants can check their laptops before registering for a workshop.

Teaching assistants

Each workshop should be supported by at least one teaching assistant (TA), who helps participants with set-up issues and with the practical exercises, so that everyone can keep pace with the milestones.

Author feedback

Authors receive feedback based on attendance: 40% of the workshop registration fees are given to the authors of the workshop. Choose an engaging topic and draw in as many participants as possible!

How proposals are evaluated

All criteria apply, with particular attention to Clarity & Feasibility — can the agenda be completed within 90 minutes? — and the Engagement Plan, the degree of meaningful interactivity planned for participants. Review criteria
